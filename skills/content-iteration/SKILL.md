# Content iteration

Turn critique into a visibly revised artifact without losing its intended audience, meaning, voice, or agreed finish line. This is the loop between review and re-review, not a replacement for content-factory (external resource; not included) production or the owning review: writing-review (external resource; not included) for substantial prose, copywriting (external resource; not included) for conversion copy, and [creative-review](../creative-review/SKILL.md) for visual or audiovisual work. A clear contained correction can go straight to its media specialist; use this workflow when feedback must be reconciled, the concept is disputed, or revisions keep missing the point.

## Generated media in this workflow

When feedback asks for richer imagery, personality, or storytelling, use [generated media and storytelling](../shared-video-references/creative-exploration.md#generated-media-and-storytelling) to revise the actual scene, metaphor, or transformation. Preserve selected subjects and visual language across regenerated stills and motion; fix a weak premise before rendering more detail. Under Content Factory, follow its human creative checkpoints (external resource; not included) for materially changed directions and representative acceptance before higher-fidelity production. A precise approved correction proceeds within scope; a new creative proposal is not already accepted.

## Inspect before changing

Identify the exact version the user is reacting to and inspect it, including the named region, passage, or timecode and its surrounding context. Preserve that baseline. Reuse the agreed audience, purpose, medium, references, product truth, and constraints. Terse visual feedback is a valid brief; do not demand the user rewrite it.

For video, use creative-review's [visual review](../creative-review/references/visual-content-review.md) and [video-editor](../video-editor/SKILL.md) for technical inspection. Frame samples may reject visible defects but cannot prove continuous motion, timing, or audio quality. Report the inspection method honestly. Never turn occluded or unobserved contact into evidence of successful physical action. If review is delegated, collect and resolve findings that bear on essential requirements before presenting the revision as fixed. An unfinished review is not a pass.

## Triage the actual rejection

Map each distinct critique to KEEP, CHANGE, or KILL, with its source and concrete consequence. Distinguish:

- **Concept/payoff:** the meaning, product relationship, or ending is wrong. Reconsider that part of the premise before dependent production. A prettier version of the same rejected setting does not answer this critique.
- **Direction:** the concept works but staging, framing, light, setting, or performance fails to express it. Revise those choices deliberately.
- **Execution:** contact, continuity, product fidelity, wording, timing, or technical output fails. Repair the affected source and check for regressions.

Try removal, simplification, and repair before adding complexity, but do not remove an essential action or payoff merely because the generator struggles. KILL retires a named creative choice, not its source files or unrelated approved work. Preserve user decisions separately from reviewer preferences and synthetic hypotheses; name consequential conflicts instead of silently averaging them.

For a side-by-side demo, use [comparison-demo packaging](../shared-video-references/comparison-demo-packaging.md) to separate a weak challenge from idle-start, crop, capture-quality, or synchronization defects. Map each correction to the actual source interval or frame and preserve comparison facts. Do not infer causal reach gains from a changed post with multiple simultaneous revisions.

## Concept before replacement assets

When the user rejects a scene or ending, use [video-script](../video-script/SKILL.md) for story, dialogue, narration, or payoff; use [video-direction](../video-direction/SKILL.md) for staging, framing, and camera choices. Use content-factory's [concept development](../shared-video-references/concept-development.md) when the underlying mechanism needs alternatives. State the human experience, visible relationship, product connection, and concrete final image. Compare a few materially different mechanisms when the choice is open and recommend one with a reason. Under Content Factory, obtain the required human selection before dependent production; otherwise follow the host review agreement. Establish visual language and scene elements from that decision. Do not reopen settled parts unnecessarily.

For marketing content whose rejected concept concerns the audience, promise, or differentiation, use positioning-angles (external resource; not included) before rewriting or producing replacement assets.

For a difficult reveal, create and inspect the replacement end-frame asset before asking animation to invent it. Compare setup and endpoint together: what specific motif or geometry carries through, what leaves frame first, and what remains unchanged? Use [image-generation-guide](../image-generation-guide/SKILL.md) for actual assets, with product/identity/composition reference roles and explicit preservation constraints. Respect a requested provider. A generated keyframe completes a keyframe request, not an outstanding finished-video request.

## Revise and re-test

For every iteration assignment, produce **at least three actual versions, progressing from low fidelity to high fidelity**, as Eric explicitly requests. Preserve and show the progression, not just the final render:

1. **Low fidelity:** a rough draft, sketch, layout, storyboard, or animatic that tests the concept and the named correction cheaply.
2. **Medium fidelity:** a developed version that tests composition, wording, staging, continuity, or timing before final polish.
3. **High fidelity:** the finished version in the requested medium, incorporating the earlier reviews and receiving its own re-test.

These are three or more new, inspectable artifact versions, not three descriptions, the old baseline counted as a version, or three polished outputs made before the concept is tested. Additional alternatives or revisions are appropriate when needed. Use the owning review at each fidelity stage, selecting only the checks relevant to the correction. Do not run both Writing Review and Creative Review on the same article because it includes diagrams. Resolve findings before increasing fidelity and record what changed and why; the finished version receives its own review. Keep the scale proportional to the task, but do not skip the progression for a small correction unless the user explicitly overrides it. For motion feedback, test motion in the rough stage with a playable animatic or clip; stills alone cannot close a motion defect. The minimum version count is not a quality pass and does not expand spending or publishing authority.

Use writer (external resource; not included) for Eric-authored voice and copywriting (external resource; not included) for conversion copy; use the media specialists above for images and video. Specialist handoffs inherit the agreed brief, scope, authorization, and three-version progression unless the user overrides it; reuse settled intake and apply only the relevant specialist steps.

Use these focused checks when the feedback calls for them:

- **Confusing writing, a buried point, or substantial reordering:** use writing-review's cold-reader and structure checks (external resource; not included), then repeat the relevant check on the revised prose.
- **Matching a supplied video reference:** use [reference-style-video](../reference-style-video/SKILL.md) to measure the reference and revision with comparable pacing, framing, caption, and sound criteria.
- **Sound, emotional timing, dialogue clarity, music, or audio transitions:** use [sound-storytelling](../sound-storytelling/SKILL.md) to revise the affected cues and review them in the complete cut.

Change the actual source, preserve versioned outputs, and keep a compact decision record in the existing project. Log feedback → decision → affected passage/frame/shot → evidence from re-test. Avoid a mandatory dashboard or review site; use review-with-me (external resource; not included) only when comparison or anchored feedback warrants it.

After repeated similar failures, change the production approach using the observed failure: prepare a better reference, change blocking, split an overloaded shot, or use another authorized tool. Do not just strengthen adjectives or keep submitting the same request. Do not substitute a different deliverable or eliminate the promised action without making that tradeoff explicit.

Re-review both the named fix and previously settled invariants. For video, inspect the assembled sequence and joins, not only the new endpoint; motion issues require playback evidence at the delivered speed, with sound when present. Successful decode proves integrity, not creative success. Collect any essential independent reviews before the final verdict. Resolve failures within scope; if a capability or authority blocks completion, clearly identify the unfinished requirement rather than delivering a known failed take with a success headline and caveat.

## Hand back the work

Lead with the revised artifact and a short account of what changed. Keep concept approval, visible execution, technical validation, full playback, and measured audience outcomes distinct. Show comparable before/after views when they clarify the change. Name unresolved defects and the specific next test; a change log or a newly written skill is not evidence that the media improved. Stop at the agreed deliverable, without publishing, spending beyond authorization, or creating indefinite iteration loops.
