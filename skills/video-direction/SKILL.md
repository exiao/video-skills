# Video direction

## Generated media in this workflow

For visual design, launch, or mixed-media briefs, proactively explore the treatments in [generated media and storytelling](../shared-video-references/creative-exploration.md#generated-media-and-storytelling): a story-bearing still, a composited loop, or a continuous transformation between states. Direct the actual start/end compositions, subject invariants, motion, and layout relationship; test the hardest transition before coverage. Keep baked background effects distinct from real-time interaction and inspect actual joins and playback. Carry Content Factory's human creative checkpoints (external resource; not included) through direction and generation handoffs; ordinary take iteration within an accepted treatment does not reopen selection.

## Direction and production

For help with tone, pacing, music, or developing an idea, read the examples from The Curse (external resource; not included). They include a rough edit used to show tone, a real incident that grew into a story, a Columbo phone call, and a restaurant location. Each has a timestamped source and advice for applying it. Choose what fits the brief.

For an open concept brief, use concept development and selection (external resource; not included) before committing to a treatment. For reveal-led films, establish the setup, final image, and what the reveal changes for the viewer, then direct the transition around those requirements. Preserve concepts already settled by the user.

For social and launch videos, apply packaging and attention (external resource; not included): compose the title/cover and first second before investing in later shots. Direct a visible reason to pause and a concrete next beat. Test the opening at phone size and muted; reject an attractive frame whose subject, tension, or promised payoff is unclear.

For side-by-side product or model demos, use [comparison-demo packaging](../shared-video-references/comparison-demo-packaging.md) to direct a readable working area, identifiable subjects, first meaningful action, and visible divergence/payoff. Choose an unusual challenge only when it reveals something useful or entertaining; preserve the integrity of any claimed comparison.

For a cinematic ad built on a dramatic analogy (external resource; not included), direct the visible wrong action and the character's recognition of it before the final interface or brand reveal. Use framing, reaction time, and a concrete prop or screen to make the product's causal role legible. Check both the full film and any placement cutdown; the reference's long runtime and delayed mark are creative choices to test, not defaults.

For still-image generation and editing, follow [$image-generation-guide](../image-generation-guide/SKILL.md). Keep this skill's creative requirements.

When the deliverable is **start and end frames for image-to-video**, deliver each shot's start and end as separate, full-frame image files at the intended aspect ratio and matching dimensions. Keep camera axis, subject scale, identity, lighting, and persistent props aligned while changing only the directed action or state. A side-by-side sheet is useful for review but is not itself a pair of image-to-video inputs. Inspect both exported files individually, record their paths under the shot ID, and check whether the selected animator actually accepts both endpoints before calling the pair production-ready.

Direction is model independent. Own visual treatment, shot breakdown, camera, lighting, blocking, visual continuity, generation prompts, and take selection. For a narrow prompt or shot revision, deliver only that direction; do not require a script first. Use [$video-script](../video-script/SKILL.md) when narrative or spoken-copy work is also requested. For a finished-video request, use the applicable production workflow, drawing on direction as needed.

Own every visible detail of the final result: deliberately specify it or consciously accept it after inspection. Exploration may leave details flexible, but final review must account for them. Use the detail-review standard (external resource; not included) to inspect composition and close details, correct mismatches, and check that edits preserve earlier decisions. Overall aesthetic success alone does not establish completion.

For tool-selection advice, use dated model options and Eric's taste (external resource; not included): he values Midjourney's default stylization for concepting and character ideation. Treat tutorial rankings as dated and verify current candidates when selection matters. This does not change the still-image generation default above or establish character continuity without testing.

Choose animation capability for the motion task, not merely because it shares the still-image provider or has an Animate button. Distinguish a provider explicitly selected for the whole production from one selected only for still concepting; preserve the former, and do not infer the latter is a motion-quality endorsement. Within available authorization, check the relevant controls and test the hardest essential motion or transition before scaling. Record the actual animator/model where exposed and why the representative take meets the brief. Repeated failures call for a changed approach or another permitted animator, not cosmetic editing that removes the payoff; ask only if the change requires new authority.

When a script exists, add stable shot IDs beneath its scene IDs in the same production document. A scene may contain several shots. Scene sections remain authoritative for spoken copy; shot records reference the relevant lines rather than maintaining another editable copy. Preserve approved story, dialogue, caption text, and essential visual beats. Flag timing conflicts and propose adjustments instead of silently rewriting approved copy. If copy changes are authorized, update the scene text and affected shot references together.

Put the visual treatment at the top and repeat its useful style constraints in each shot. For production handoffs, record relevant framing, movement, action, environment, lighting, duration, sound cues, references, continuity, and acceptance notes. Omit fields that add no useful direction. For direction-only work, a compact shot list is sufficient. When handing work to another maker or reviewer, use the assignment requirements in [production coordination](references/production-coordination.md), even if no separate role is needed. Put them in the existing production document or assignment rather than creating a second template.

Develop open visual treatments in the order **human concept → visual language → scene elements**. State the recognizable experience or tension, choose visual decisions that embody it, then specify the shot's action, posture, setting, and props. Carry an existing approved concept forward. Beauty, an attractive subject, and coherent styling can support the work but do not establish emotional resonance. Review whether the actual frame or sequence makes the human meaning legible through visible relationships and behavior, separately from polish and technical quality. A still can imply a story without a full arc. Read concept before aesthetic (external resource; not included) for concept review criteria; its examples illustrate reasoning rather than prescribe a creative direction.

For a treatment developed from references, pair the human concept with three guiding words distilled from them. Translate the words into concrete visual choices and use them to check coherence during take selection; attractive choices that undermine the intention need revision. Follow the concept guidance (external resource; not included) for the worked example and intentional-contrast allowance. These words belong in the existing treatment, not as a substitute for specific shot direction.

Before drafting generation prompts, establish the treatment's meaningful lighting (quality, source, direction), palette and saturation, and camera choices (framing, position, distance, field of view, focus, and movement). Connect each to the story, especially how much setting must remain visible for the concept to read. Use visual-language guidance (external resource; not included) when developing these choices. Lens numbers and film-stock names are cues; judge the visible result and preserve room for deliberate revision after trials.

Coherence can include deliberate contrast between rendering styles. Define what each treatment contributes and preserve intentional differences while checking spatial and temporal integration.

Reuse supplied references and prepare additional product, character, location, prop, or spatial references only when needed for continuity or an authorized generation task. Compare alternatives with one variable changed at a time and keep the strongest candidates until motion proves which one holds. Prepare separate references for deliberate mid-scene changes. A short generated shot is usually safer than asking one generation to carry a long sequence; join separate clips in post.

For demanding concept work, expect sustained iteration—Eric anticipates 10–50+ image candidates and edits when needed to reach the intended frame. Use the iteration and production-stage guidance (external resource; not included): explore, inspect, select, and make targeted corrections while preserving successful details. A first attractive image is not the finish line; stop when the brief is met, or report the specific remaining gap if an actual resource limit intervenes. Keep ordinary authorized rounds moving without repeated approval checkpoints.

For generated sequences, establish a few distinctive anchor frames, character references, and spatial relationships before filling coverage. Test a representative frame in motion before expanding the look; a beautiful still may animate poorly. Keep direction specific through chosen materials, light, props, and behavior rather than generic cinematic adjectives. Use shared frames or small angle groups when they help continuity, checking face detail and composition at delivery size. Treat grid sizes, upscalers, model rankings, and long-versus-short shot preferences as workload-dependent choices to verify, not permanent rules from a tutorial. Preserve pacing, reactions, and breathing room for performance. Read AI creative judgment (external resource; not included) when synthetic realism or story credibility is at issue.

Sources: [Patchwright workflow (2026-05-13)](https://pjace.beehiiv.com/p/gossip-goblin-s-crazy-workflow-for-building-original-worlds-200m-views) discusses anchor images and distinctive world details; [Hellgrind and Mork workflow (2026-04-25)](https://pjace.beehiiv.com/p/you-re-using-seedance-2-0-wrong-try-this-massive-time-saver-instead) discusses motion trials and spatial continuity; [four-shot reference workflow (2026-01-09)](https://pjace.beehiiv.com/p/i-regret-nothing-except-reading-the-comments) offers a dated small-grid technique. These are dated production accounts, not universal tool benchmarks; verify techniques on the actual output.

Write prompts as temporal action, not a pile of adjectives. Default to scene elements first (subject/action, environment, styling, props), followed by visual language (light, color, camera, framing, texture), with the concept woven throughout and beginning/middle/end motion specified. An optional closing sentence can reinforce the intended feeling. See treatment-to-prompt guidance (external resource; not included); adapt ordering to explicit user or tool-specific formats. Describe the physical actions that matter; avoid overconstraining incidental motion. Generate text, logos, UI, and subtitles in post because video models commonly render them unreliably.

This skill plans and reviews direction. It may use an available media-generation capability when the user requests generation and the relevant tool is present, but it must not invent a provider CLI, install a global tool, spend credits, publish, or submit a paid generation outside the user's scope. If no generation capability is available, return the complete shot list and prompts for handoff. Review the actual media before calling a take good; inspect motion, identity, continuity, audio, and the intended cut.

Read [references/shot-list-workflow.md](references/shot-list-workflow.md) for the canonical shot-list fields and revision loop, and [references/prompting.md](references/prompting.md) for compact prompt language. For recurring casts, sets, props, or controlled variants, use [reusable reference packs](references/prompting.md#reusable-reference-packs) for reference ownership, state selection, and a three-shot worked example.
For short-form openings, read [references/short-form-first-frame.md](references/short-form-first-frame.md) to direct the first visual action alongside the spoken hook.

For a weak take or generic-looking frame, diagnose model fit, visual decisions, and concept separately (external resource; not included). Make consequential lighting, camera, composition, and blocking choices deliberate. Match the revision to the observed gap before changing tools or generating more takes.

## Optional production roles

For a substantial production or a request for separate story, creative, and technical reviews, use [production coordination](references/production-coordination.md). Keep direction and canonical-document ownership in the main task. Load only the role briefs needed for a bounded assignment; they are not mandatory stages or separately configured agents. A small edit can use [$video-editor](../video-editor/SKILL.md) directly, and script or audiovisual critique belongs with [$creative-review](../creative-review/SKILL.md). Standalone article critique belongs with $writing-review (external resource; not included).
