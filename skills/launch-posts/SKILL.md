# Launch Posts

Turn a real product change into one concise social post and a finished product video. Match the voice to the chosen author or account, the product's actual state, and the requested destination. References guide composition and motion, not current product claims.

## Find the announcement

- Verify what changed from the available release record, product surface, screenshots, or other primary evidence. Separate built, submitted, approved, and publicly live states. Check the destination and any numbers, locales, dates, URLs, or feature claims used in copy. If a claim cannot be verified, omit it or label it for review.
- Choose one reader-facing takeaway and one concrete proof. For a screenshot refresh, show what the screens let a person understand or do; a count of uploaded images is supporting context, not the whole story.
- For a post attributed to Eric, use writer (external resource; not included) to preserve his voice; for a brand account, use its established voice. Open with the actual change, show specific examples when supported, and close with a relevant action or link if there is one. Match the platform's length and tone. Remove internal production labels, self-congratulation, and generic launch hype.
- Before rendering, verify the intended public destination, any URL and closing text, and whether the post announces a submission, approval, or public release. Where the video, post, or follow-up names a destination, check that each points to the intended place and describes the same release state.

## Default deliverable

- A normal launch-posts request includes both copy and a composed product video, even when the user does not explicitly say "animation". Produce the video before declaring the post complete; do not stop at copy, a storyboard, or a trimmed recording.
- Use [animation-creator](../animation-creator/SKILL.md) with [claude-cli](../claude-cli/SKILL.md), choosing Opus 5.5 for motion design. Use [video-editor](../video-editor/SKILL.md) to finish the export. If that route is unavailable, report the limitation rather than silently substituting a plain recording.
- Explicit requests for copy only, a still, an unedited walkthrough, or another format override this default. Do not ask whether to make a video when the default already settles that choice.

## Build the visual

- Before choosing the treatment, inspect the strongest relevant approved launch video available in the conversation, linked chats, or supplied artifacts. Do this without waiting for the user to name the reference again. Compare playback at feed size: opening seconds, aspect ratio, framing scale, scene variety, transition rhythm, motion within the product, and ending. Use [reference-style-video](../reference-style-video/SKILL.md) for the beat sheet. Transfer its useful editing grammar into this product's own imagery and identity; do not copy unrelated copy, duration, or palette.
- With or without a reference, start from a compact product-story structure: a distinctive reveal that shows the payoff quickly; two or three varied, large feature views that progress through a real entry, action, and result; then a deliberate closing composition. Make at least one action happen within the captured product UI rather than moving only the whole screenshot. Vary scale, framing, and transition rhythm according to the content. If the launch supports only one strong feature, use fewer scenes and give that action room to read. The approved Bible app and Bloom widget revisions illustrate this treatment; their 12-second 4:5 format is a reference choice, not a universal requirement.
- A screen recording is source footage, not automatically the finished launch video. Crop out idle phone space and unrelated UI, integrate the useful action into the composition, and show its result. A recording trim or repeated screenshot zooms alone do not satisfy the default.
- Use real UI captures, screenshots, or footage that represent the change being announced. Show one legible product change in the first few seconds. Keep text and controls readable at ordinary feed size; use new marketing headings only when they clarify what the viewer sees.
- For a screenshot tour, choose a visual hierarchy rather than repeating the same full-screen card and slow push for every beat. A layered opener, larger feature view, or grouped close can help when each serves the story. Animate existing interface elements when that makes the change clearer: a real verse state can advance, a card can move forward, or an alert can arrive. Use exact source crops and captured states so moving UI settles into truthful pixels. Preserve text, numbers, controls, and meaning; do not fabricate product behavior or leave doubled text and ghost controls. If the source lacks a needed shot, obtain a real capture or revise the concept.
- Build and inspect a playable rough, then a developed full cut, then the corrected final export. Keep these actual versions and editable source. On feedback, use [content-iteration](../content-iteration/SKILL.md) to carry the correction through that progression. Inspect representative encoded frames and joins; a contact sheet or successful decode alone cannot establish good timing. Check legibility, clipping, duplicate overlays, and aspect ratio, then fix visible defects. Reject a cut that is essentially repeated full-screen cards with slow zooms, even if its export is technically valid; revise the composition or capture an actual product action before calling it finished.
- Watch the cut at ordinary feed speed, paying special attention to its first few seconds. If the viewer cannot read the change before the next scene, shorten or simplify the sequence. A short single reveal or an after-only view may communicate more than a multi-scene comparison. Remove labels and title cards that repeat what the product footage already shows.
- If the direction is genuinely open, make a small storyboard or sample beat so the user can choose the premise. Once a direction is selected, carry it through without reopening routine editing choices. Use content-factory (external resource; not included) for a larger campaign or multiple coordinated assets.

### Add generated media when it strengthens the idea

- For an open visual brief, consider one story-bearing still, restrained loop, or transformation that makes the product change easier to feel or understand. State what the viewer sees change and why that belongs to this product. Use [generated media and storytelling](../shared-video-references/creative-exploration.md#generated-media-and-storytelling) for the treatment; skip generation when a real capture already tells the story clearly.
- Use [image-generation-guide](../image-generation-guide/SKILL.md) for generated stills and reference frames. Brief the scene, composition, crop, copy space, and each reference's role. Inspect the actual image at feed size before integrating it. Keep logos, exact UI, figures, and post text as real assets or composited layers when fidelity matters.
- For generated video, use [video-direction](../video-direction/SKILL.md) to define the first and last frames, action, camera, and continuity, then test the essential motion in a short sample before producing the full cut. Use an available generation tool within the current authorization and [video-editor](../video-editor/SKILL.md) for assembly. Inspect the rendered motion, joins, identity, and readable overlays; a good still does not establish a good video.
- Keep generated scenes visibly illustrative. Show real product captures for factual demonstrations and do not present synthetic UI, customers, results, or interactions as observed evidence. Generation does not itself authorize paid credits, publishing, or a change to an already selected creative direction.

## Save and verify

- Completion requires the inspected composed video as well as the post copy, unless the user selected an exception above. Before attaching rendered media, confirm the export has finished: check the expected duration, dimensions, frame count, and full decode of the final file. A file that exists while encoding is still running may be incomplete.
- When the user requests a platform draft, use its specialist tool, such as typefully (external resource; not included). Select the intended account from current context or ask if ambiguous. Create or update the requested draft with the inspected media, then fetch it to verify copy, account, public destination, and status. Compare the saved media ID with the upload response for the final inspected export, especially after a revision; an earlier render attached to the draft is not the final asset. On an ambiguous write result, inspect the destination before retrying.
- When revising a draft, preserve its account, post text, and other approved fields unless the user asks to change them. Update the existing draft, then fetch it again; a media replacement should not create a second post or silently alter the copy.
- Drafting does not authorize scheduling or publication. Follow the user's existing authorization for those actions, and verify the resulting state. Report the post link or local preview plus the exact state: concept, exported, inspected, drafted, scheduled, published, or measured.
