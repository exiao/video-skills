# Visual-content review

Review the actual still or video against the intended audience, response, concept, and placement. Use AI creative judgment (external resource; not included) when generated imagery, visual metaphor, or fictional credibility is relevant. Preserve the brief's artistic intent; do not impose realism, conflict, comedy, or a sales CTA on every piece.

## Still images

Identify what the viewer can infer from the frame itself: a relationship, gesture, contradiction, demonstration, or invitation to imagine. Name the visible evidence for that reading. Assess whether framing, scale, light, color, styling, and subject behavior make the concept legible at the intended viewing size. A beautiful subject or coherent palette alone does not establish meaning; a single visual relationship can supply the whole payoff without a narrative arc.

For product content, assess whether the product's actual feature, form, motif, or use contributes to the idea. Compare supplied references when fidelity matters. Deliberately mixed rendering styles can serve one coherent intention; distinguish that choice from accidental mismatches. Use image-generation-guide for technical image inspection where needed, keeping reference deviations and anatomy defects separate from optional aesthetic preferences.

## Video

Watch the complete cut when available. Assess how the opening sets an expectation, how action and editing develop it, and whether the actual ending pays it off. Name the specific reveal rather than accepting “a surprising ending” as a resolved idea. Evaluate performance, pauses, pacing, sound, and framing by what they contribute to meaning and attention, without requiring a fixed hook length or story formula.

Use video-direction and video-editor for relevant technical checks of motion, identity, spatial continuity, cuts, captions, sound mix, and export. Tie problems to timecodes. A transcript supports spoken-content review; sampled frames support limited visual observations. Neither establishes complete playback, acting, or audible quality. State what was watched or heard and leave unavailable checks unverified.

Before a final-video keep verdict, review the complete assembled cut at playback speed, with sound if present, then inspect suspect moments frame by frame. Name the file/version, review method, and consequential timecoded findings in the existing production record. If available tools only expose still samples, use them to reject visible failures but mark motion, pacing, and final creative approval incomplete; opening a player or exporting a contact sheet is not evidence of watching the cut. Do not claim a full review through a delegate that only ran metadata or decode checks.

For generated motion, check the intended style's physical and temporal logic: contact with the environment, acceleration, weight, gestures, object permanence, and texture stability. A subject translating across a background can fail the intended action even when its anatomy remains intact. Deliberate flat or puppet-like animation is valid when it serves the brief; unchosen sliding, warping, or texture swimming is not excused by stylization.

Compare the [directed action](../../video-direction/references/prompting.md#direct-the-action-through-time) with what actually happens: does the gesture complete, does its result persist, and does the reaction follow its trigger at a readable pace? Record any consequential mismatch as expected behavior versus observed behavior at the shot/timecode in the existing review notes. For narrative sequences, compare the viewer's changing understanding with the shot's planned purpose; check that staging, sound, or an early insert has not accidentally disclosed the reveal. For atmospheric sequences, judge the intended feeling and progression without imposing a plot.

For a sequence using a [shared reference pack](../../video-direction/references/prompting.md#reusable-reference-packs), review each assigned responsibility during playback and across cuts: face and wardrobe, product shape and count, prop state, and room layout where visible. Check selected open/closed or before/after states against the intended action; distinguish a planned change from drift. Two views on a reference sheet should not produce two physical objects unless the scene calls for them. Record the failed responsibility, shot/timecode, and source reference/version so the maker can repair the affected shot and recheck its joins. Mark obscured details unverified. For controlled variants, confirm the intended fixed elements actually stayed fixed before attributing differences to the changed hook or action.

For a reveal, ask what visible continuity lets the viewer recognize the revealed object as the source of the opening world. Compare story-bearing motifs, geometry, scale, and product construction across the join. A dissolve between different patterns, or a reversed camera move toward an unrelated product shot, does not by itself establish that relationship. Cuts, reversals, and dissolves are valid techniques only when the resulting sequence earns the intended payoff. If that essential connection fails, return revise even if both shots are attractive. Recheck the repaired sequence, not just its new endpoint.

For a requested video recreation, also use [aligned source/output comparison](../../reference-style-video/references/video-recreation.md#compare-before-calling-it-recreated): test retained cut times, framing, action beats and audio separately from replacement identity and setting. A coherent new video may still fail the requested recreation.

To review tone, use the rough-edit example (external resource; not included): do the images, acting, timing, and sound create the intended feeling? To review location choices, use the restaurant's homemade wood-and-plexiglass barriers (external resource; not included). Explain what a detail shows about the place and whether it blocks an important action. Random clutter and generation errors do not make a scene believable.

## Collections and revision

For a connected campaign, review the assets together: shared idea, product representation, tone, and each piece's distinct contribution. Purposeful variation can be coherent; repeated styling and near-duplicates do not by themselves make a campaign. Do not force unrelated test concepts into one visual system.

Return keep, revise, or rethink with the strongest evidence and fewest consequential changes. Route an empty or unsuitable premise back to concept development; route unclear staging, missing beats, fidelity failures, or technical defects to the relevant maker while preserving the viable concept. If revision is authorized, inspect the changed artifact again. Use artifact-review only when a comparison or unresolved human judgment merits an interactive review surface; it presents choices and captures feedback, not a substitute for this critique.

## Vertical short-form placement

Use [Reels placement keyframes](../../reference-style-video/references/reels-placement.md) when assessing talking-head composition, titles, subtitles, or graphics. Check title inset, eyeline, face/action clearance, and the intended interface overlay separately. Tie a placement finding to a frame or timecode and distinguish an illustrated safe area from verified platform clearance. Preserve purposeful framing; do not treat a creator example as evidence of engagement lift.
