# Creative review

Give an evidence-backed creative judgment and the revisions that would most improve the requested piece. Review is read-only by default: identify problem passages, visible details, or timecoded beats and propose specific changes. Apply revisions when requested or already authorized by the parent production task; review alone does not authorize publishing or changing an external document.

Choose by what carries the experience. An article with explanatory diagrams belongs to writing-review (external resource; not included), including its essential visuals. A video with narration and captions belongs here, including its words. An article's cover and feed preview belong here; its body belongs to Writing Review. Use copywriting (external resource; not included) for standalone conversion copy and hooks (external resource; not included) for a focused headline or opening-line task.

Review the requested artifact once with its owning skill. Use both creative and writing reviews only for distinct deliverables, such as an article and its promotional video. Writing Room owns the writing and revision workflow, not a second creative verdict.

Inspect the supplied artifact and relevant brief before judging it: read the complete draft, view the actual image, or watch the video with sound when available. For linked material, retrieve the actual content rather than judging a search snippet or generated summary; state inaccessible portions or sensory limits. Use the intended audience, surface, purpose, and author's voice or creative direction from context. If something material is missing, ask a focused question or label a reasonable provisional assumption.

## Generated media in this workflow

For generated visual work, apply generated media and storytelling (external resource; not included) to judge the specific scene, relationship, transformation, or reveal. Preserve deliberate atmosphere, wonder, humor, and emotional storytelling when they serve the brief; nonfunctional does not mean unnecessary. Identify generic spectacle and claims the media falsely implies. Inspect in-context imagery and actual motion as appropriate, separating strong keyframes from successful continuity/playback. An agent's keep verdict does not replace the user's answer at Content Factory's human creative checkpoints (external resource; not included).

## Attention review for social video

Use packaging and attention (external resource; not included) for social and launch-video critique. Start with title/cover and the actual first second, then inspect five, ten, thirty, and sixty seconds where those points exist. At each checkpoint state the viewer's reason to continue and the visible evidence earning it. A generic promise, dead opening, or missing payoff requires revision even if typography, motion, and export checks pass. Do not postpone an attention failure behind cosmetic findings or claim retention without audience data.

For a reveal-led piece, ask: does the reveal change how we understand the opening, and does that change matter to the story or product? Identify the initial assumption, the visible detail that challenges it, and the payoff that explains it. Check continuity between the two interpretations; flag a cosmetic style change or unrelated surprise when it substitutes for a meaningful reveal. Use the [shared witness and reversal example](../video-script/references/short-form-hooks.md#witness-assumption-and-reversal) when relevant, without requiring this pattern in direct demos or straightforward explanations.

## Shared review core

Establish the audience and intended response, state the concept in one sentence, then assess whether the execution makes it legible and delivers the promised payoff. The payoff may be insight, utility, comedy, recognition, wonder, or escape; a still need not have a full plot and an informative piece need not manufacture emotion. Judge against the brief, not a universal taste standard.

Separate a weak premise from a strong premise poorly expressed. Keep conceptual resonance, visual or editorial execution, and technical defects distinguishable. Own the judgment about meaning and audience experience; use image/video specialists for relevant fidelity, anatomy, motion, continuity, sound, and export checks. Incorporate their observed findings without treating successful rendering as creative success. Qualitative review is not measured audience response.

## Words within creative work

Judge narration, captions, dialogue, and accompanying copy by how they work with the images, action, sound, and payoff. Check whether they clarify the concept, preserve the intended voice, and make only supported claims. Preserve purposeful repetition, unusual wording, and silence; do not polish away performance or humor. A script can support a story judgment but cannot prove the final acting, timing, or sound works.

For AI-led concepts or scripts, use AI creative judgment (external resource; not included) when relevant. Identify the actual laugh, learning, or chosen story payoff and whether it survives without novelty about AI. Distinguish clearly fictional emotion from fabricated lived experience. For ads, inspect whether the product belongs in the causal story rather than only the end card. Flag empty spectacle, unearned sentiment, and unsupported performance claims with the specific beat that fails. A script review does not establish that timing, acting, or sound works in the final media.

## Focused review modes

- For summaries of visual lessons or demonstrations, use visual summaries (external resource; not included). Check whether the reader sees or hears the evidence needed to understand and apply the lesson: essential examples, comparisons, motion, and sound must survive compression in a form compatible with the requested format. Accurate prose alone may miss the brief; decorative images do not repair missing evidence.

- For still images or finished video, read [visual-content review](references/visual-content-review.md). Review accompanying copy as part of the visual experience.
- For short-form scripts and hooks in a visual or audiovisual piece, read [surface-specific review](references/surface-specific-review.md).

## Useful output

Separate substantive findings (claim, evidence, structure, reader promise) from voice preferences (wording, rhythm, tone). Use the project's editorial standards for the former and the intended author's voice guide for the latter. A voice preference is not evidence of a faulty argument, and fluent prose does not repair unsupported claims.

Lead with a short verdict relative to the brief: **keep**, **revise**, or **rethink**. Then give the few highest-impact changes, each tied to a passage, visible detail, or timecoded beat and a reason. Identify whether each calls for concept development or production correction. Include what should survive revision when it helps prevent over-editing. Use `CUT: passage — reason` or `REVISE: passage → proposed alternative` when useful; propose no change if none earns its place. A keep verdict applies to the inspected stage, not unobserved final media or permission to publish.

Use qualitative labels such as strong, needs work, or weak only when comparison helps. If the user asks for scores, define the rubric and tie each score to evidence; do not use an arbitrary total as permission to publish. Match feedback length to the artifact: a headline may need only a few lines. Distinguish factual defects, missed brief requirements, and optional taste choices.

When rewriting, preserve facts, point of view, certainty, and genuine voice. Mark missing support or write around the gap; never manufacture statistics, testimonials, guarantees, or personal stories to make copy stronger. Recheck the revised piece against the original brief, not just the critique.
