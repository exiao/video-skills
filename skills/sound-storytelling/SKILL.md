# Sound storytelling

Read the treatment, script, available cut, and shot list. Decide what the audience should hear and feel before generating tracks when voice timing or sound motivates the image. Preserve the requested scope and existing authorization.

For each relevant shot record its ID, listening viewpoint, exact dialogue, actor intention, voice qualities, effects, atmosphere, music, silence, and cue times. Track sounds across cuts. Identify the foreground element; try sparse sound before adding layers. Do not use score to conceal unclear dramatic motivation.

Say what the music should add: follow the action, express a character's feelings, make the image feel unsettling, or create a mood of its own. See the music discussion and suggested comparison (external resource; not included). If unsure, try different music and a version without music, then listen to the scene.

Use sound bridges deliberately: an incoming sound can anticipate the next image, an outgoing sound can carry emotion across a cut, and offscreen sound can establish space without another shot. Preserve continuity of room tone and perspective unless the change is intentional.

For short product motion or AI-assisted UGC, read [short-form audio](references/short-form-audio.md) for a timeline-linked cue example, voice direction and a muted-message check.

For planning, return shot-linked cue notes and unresolved choices. For requested production, use available voice, music, and editing capabilities to deliver separate tracks and a mixed cut with actual file paths and timing. Use $musicgen-cli (external resource; not included) for its supported music service and [$video-editor](../video-editor/SKILL.md) for assembly when appropriate. Preserve prior mixes and record the cut version. Do not imply a planning request authorizes paid generation; reuse existing production authorization rather than asking again. Confirm consent before cloning an identifiable person's voice.

Review the complete cut for intelligibility, synchronization, transitions, silence, and the balance between speech and score. Match loudness and channels to the delivery brief; do not impose one target on all platforms. Technical probes, transcripts, and waveforms establish technical properties, not listening quality. If listening is unavailable, label perceptual quality unverified and still report technical checks. Review notes identify cut version, timestamp, shot ID, and audible problem. Revise affected cues and check adjacent transitions.
