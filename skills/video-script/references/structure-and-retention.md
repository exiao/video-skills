# Structure and retention

Short-form pieces often use: hook, setup, escalating proof or demonstration, payoff, and a specific close. Give each spoken beat a visual reason to continue watching. Rehook after a topic shift with a new question, visual, consequence, or example. Cut on the line, breath, or action when that supports comprehension; do not add random motion to compensate for a weak idea.

Long-form pieces need a clear promise, a map of sections, progressive stakes, concrete examples, and a resolved ending. Audit the first minute, section openings, repeated points, unsupported claims, and the distance between setup and payoff. Narrative pieces use character desire, obstacle, choice, consequence, and change; their pacing comes from scene turns rather than forced social hooks.

Timing is evidence. Keep timecodes continuous, leave room for breaths and transitions, and revise copy when narration cannot fit naturally. Retention tactics should serve understanding and the audience's promise, not become a checklist applied to every format.

## Progression rather than repeated setup

For attention-led social and long-form work, describe the state change in each major beat: what has been attempted, discovered, ruled out, gained, or put at stake? Two adjacent beats that merely repeat the premise usually need combining or replacing. Compress repetitive mechanics while preserving the evidence and causality needed to follow the result. A sequence of screenshots or facts is not progression merely because the visuals change.

When a format benefits from escalation, choose a meaningful axis: harder tests, deeper constraints, sharper contrasts, or increasingly consequential results. Reveal enough early progress to reward watching, then renew the question through the next consequence. Do not import the source's three- and six-minute re-engagement timestamps into every runtime. An emotional pause or a complete explanation can be the right beat; escalating price or adding random spectacle is not required.

The final beat should resolve the opening question; remove advance wrap-up signals and redundant recap when they invite viewers to leave before that resolution. Preserve a requested instructional summary, necessary qualification, or CTA when it has a real audience job.
